# Survey of the aircraft and of the pilot: for the flight model and the pilot's seat

**Nothing in the flight model or in the pilot's seat is measured today.** Every number is the handbook's,
derived from the handbook's, or estimated, and says which. This page is how a survey of one real aircraft
is recorded, checked and fed in. The file that receives it, `data/flight_survey.json`, has no record in it.

A survey does two different things:

- **A measurement on the ground, or of the pilot in the seat, replaces a number.** That number, and only
  that number, then reads *measured* in the table of the model's numbers. Three numbers of the flight model
  can be replaced this way, because each can be read off the aircraft and nothing else was fitted through it:
  the elevator's travel up and down, and the speed of the flaps. In the seat: where the eye is, how far the
  arm reaches, and where the shoulders are.
- **An observation in flight is compared with the model**, flown at the same weight, altitude and
  temperature. It replaces nothing. Whether to refit the model to the aircraft is a decision to take with
  the comparison in hand: most of the model's numbers were fitted together to the handbook's tables, and one
  of them cannot be moved alone.

The dimensions of the drawn model (span, lengths, positions) have their own survey:
[04_aircraft_measurements.md](04_aircraft_measurements.md). The two files are separate because the first
rebuilds the 3D model and this one does not.

## Recording a survey

1. Write the registration and the serial number of the aircraft in `data/flight_survey.json`. A measurement
   of an aircraft that is not identified cannot be verified.
2. Copy `data/flight_survey_template.csv` and fill in a row for each thing measured: the value, the
   tolerance (how far off the reading may be), the conditions the row asks for, how it was measured, the
   date (`YYYY-MM-DD`) and who measured it. A row left without a value was not measured and is ignored.
   Measure a thing twice as two rows with two names; do not average by hand.
3. Leave new rows `raw`. A second person checks the reading, the unit and the conditions against the
   aircraft's papers and marks the row `verified`. A wrong reading is marked `rejected`, not deleted.
4. Import, check and compare:

```bash
node 08_realistic_digital_twin/scripts/flight_survey.mjs import my_survey.csv     # into data/flight_survey.json
node 08_realistic_digital_twin/scripts/flight_survey.mjs validate
node 08_realistic_digital_twin/scripts/flight_survey.mjs report                   # verified records beside the model
node 08_realistic_digital_twin/scripts/flight_survey.mjs report --include-raw     # a preview during the fieldwork
```

A value outside what is plausible for the type is refused with the reason: it is most often a unit or a
transcription error. Raw and rejected records never change the model.

The viewer reads `data/flight_survey.json` when it starts. Verified ground and seat measurements replace
their numbers there, and **Flight → The numbers of the model and their sources** shows them as *measured*
with the name of the record.

After a measured eye point is fed in, run the browser test (`npm run test:e2e8`): it checks that the cabin of
the model stays clear of the eye wherever the head can go.

## Safety

The flight observations are ordinary training manoeuvres and performance checks, flown within the
handbook's limits, at a safe altitude, by a pilot qualified to fly them or with an instructor. Nothing here
asks for a manoeuvre the handbook does not describe. Stalling speeds read from the airspeed indicator are
rough: near the stall the indicator reads low and differs between aircraft. The comparison converts the
model's calibrated speed with the calibration table of the handbook (Figure 5-1), and a difference of a few
knots there says little.

### What to measure

**On the ground, engine off**

| Record as | What | How | Unit | Note with it | In the model |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `elevator_up_deg` | Elevator travel up | Inclinometer on the elevator. Zero it with the elevator faired with the stabilizer, then hold the control wheel fully back and read the angle. | degrees (20 to 32) | — | replaces `FM.elevatorUpDeg` |
| `elevator_down_deg` | Elevator travel down | As above, with the control wheel fully forward. | degrees (15 to 28) | — | replaces `FM.elevatorDownDeg` |
| `flap_time_s` | Time for the flaps to travel from up to 40° | Stopwatch, on the ground, master switch on, engine off. Note the bus voltage in the notes. | seconds (4 to 20) | — | replaces `FM.flapRateDegS` |

**The pilot in the seat**

| Record as | What | How | Unit | Note with it | In the model |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## What happens to each kind of record

| Kind | Status | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Ground or seat | verified | Replaces its number; the number reads *measured*, citing the record. Several verified records of one thing give their mean. |
| Flight | verified | Shown beside the model at the same conditions, with the difference and whether it is within the tolerance of the reading. |
| Any | raw | Ignored, except in a report asked with `--include-raw`. |
| Any | rejected | Kept for the record, never used. |
